# V'gine — product lexicon note

**Status:** approved product-owned lexicon entry  
**Scope:** name history / product terminology  
**Canonical label:** `V'gine`

## One-line definition

**DE:** V'gine ist der Name des Graph1ks Sound-Studios; die Schreibweise hat einen realen historischen Vorläufer als spätmittelalterliche Kürzung von `virgine`.

**EN:** V'gine is the Graph1ks sound-studio name; its spelling has a documented historical precedent as a late-medieval abbreviation of `virgine`.

## Plain definition

The spelling is not invented historical cosplay. Forms written `v'gine` are attested in late-medieval manuscript and early-print material and are expanded by editors/cataloguers as `virgine`.

In Latin, `virgine` is the ablative singular of `virgo`, a noun meaning a maiden or virgin. Religious material frequently uses the form in Marian contexts.

This historical fact is the relevant product-lore connection. It does **not** establish one universal pronunciation for the modern product name: pronunciation varied with language, period and reading tradition, and the V'gine brand pronunciation is a separate product convention.

## Evidence notes

- *Het liedboek van Liisbet Ghoeyuaers*, editorial account: the abbreviation `v'gine` is explicitly reported as completed to `virgine`.
- W. A. Copinger, *Supplement to Hain's Repertorium bibliographicum*, entry for a Bologna printing dated 18 April 1492, transcribes `seraphicha v'gine sancta Chaterina da Siena`.
- *Der Münchner Oswald* describes a mid-fifteenth-century manuscript and transcribes a Latin devotional ownership/note context containing `v'gine`.
- Lewis & Short, *A Latin Dictionary*, `virgo`: “a maid, maiden, virgin”; `virgine` is the ablative singular form.

## Product-language rule

Use the historical connection as a concise lexicon fact, not as a claim that the modern product is itself a medieval word with one fixed spelling, meaning or pronunciation.
